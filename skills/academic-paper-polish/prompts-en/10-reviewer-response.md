# Module 10 · Responding to Reviewers

> **How to use**: copy the code block below, replace the placeholder, send to any LLM. Self-contained.

```text
I have received reviewer comments. Help me reply point by point.

**Reply principles:**
1. Every question must be answered individually — no merging, no skipping.
2. Run the requested additional experiments wherever possible.
3. Where you cannot, do not avoid it: state the honest reason.
4. Papers recommended by reviewers must be cited and discussed thoroughly.
5. Understand the comment from the reviewer's perspective. If you decline a requested
   change, argue it on the evidence — do not fight the reviewer.
6. Thank the editor and the reviewers.

**Structure of each reply (four elements):**
- Thanks (specific to the point raised)
- Action taken (new experiment / revised argument / added reference)
- 🔴 State which line of the original text changed, to what — quote before and after
- Corresponding supplementary-material numbering (e.g. new figure moved to Fig. S5)

**Output format:**
Comment N: [reviewer's comment verbatim]
Response: [your reply]
Changes in manuscript: [which line, before vs after]

Reviewer comments: [PASTE]
Relevant manuscript passages: [PASTE]
```

## 🔴 The four elements (worked example)

Reviewer: *"N2 purging may not remove all O2; please provide post-treatment dissolved oxygen data."*

```
→ Thanks for this concern. As suggested, we supplemented the experiments to monitor
  the variation of dissolved O2 concentration during N2 purging. Measurement showed
  that the concentration of dissolved O2 rapidly decreased to 0.27 mg/L within 10 min
  and then slowly decreased to around 0.1 mg/L (Fig. R1).

→ To address the reviewer's concern, the original statement "the solution was purged
  by N2 (99.999%) for at least 30 min to remove O2" was revised to "the solution was
  purged by N2 (99.999%) for at least 30 min to reduce the dissolved O2 concentration
  to below 0.1 mg/L (Fig. S5)." (Lines 193–195)

→ In addition, the Fig. R1 was copied as Fig. S5 in the revised supporting information.
```

**Four elements**: ① thanks → ② action taken with data → ③ **which line changed, to what** → ④ supplementary-material numbering

## 🔴 Six disciplines (check each)

| # | Discipline | Consequence of violation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Answer every point individually** | The editor concludes you are uncooperative |
| 2 | Run the requested experiments where possible | Appears perfunctory |
| 3 | If you cannot, **do not dodge** — give the honest reason | Dodging looks evasive |
| 4 | **Cite the papers reviewers recommend**, and discuss them thoroughly | Ignoring advice |
| 5 | **Do not fight the reviewer** | Even when you are right, the reviewer has the final word |
| 6 | Thank the editor and reviewers | Basic courtesy |

## 🔴 What reviewers check (reverse self-audit)

1. Have you **appropriately cited your own closely related work**?
2. Is the topic **suitable for the journal**?
3. Is the content **novel and important**?
4. Is the argument logical; are the discussion and conclusion sound?
5. Are the references appropriate?
6. Is the expression correct, concise, and clear?
7. Is the experimental description clear and **reproducible**; are the data real and reliable?
8. Are the figures and tables necessary, standard, and clear?

## Finding reviewers

International journals generally ask for **3–5 suggested reviewers**:
- Scientists in your field found via SCI search
- Authors in your reference list
- Editorial board members of relevant journals
- Conference chairs/committee members; professionals you know

⚠️ **Do not suggest field luminaries for an ordinary paper.** Build and maintain your own reviewer pool.

---

## Major vs minor revision

| | Major revision | Minor revision |
|---|---|---|
| Meaning | Substantial work, **re-review** | Small fixes; **effectively accept** |
| Typical deadline | 2–3 months | 1–2 weeks |
| Strategy | Address every point; treat reviewers as collaborators | Respond quickly; do not stall |

**The worst response**: changing only the wording, not the substance. Editors can tell you are paying lip service.
