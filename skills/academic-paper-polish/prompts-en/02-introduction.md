- Gap argument

---

## 🔴 The hardest rule: opening must match resolution

The most common failure of an introduction is not poor writing — it is **a mismatch between the question posed and the answer delivered**.

| Shape | Term | Reader reaction |
|---|---|---|
| Opening wider than resolution | *overpromising* | Feels cheated |
| **Opening = resolution** | ***on target*** | **Satisfied** |
| Resolution wider than opening | *underpromising* | No story worth following |

---

## 🔴 Citation density

**Every sentence of the introduction needs a citation, except the problem statement and transitional sentences.**

| Error | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Missing citations | Reviewers will **at least conclude that you have not read enough** |
| No clear question stated | Scientific significance is unclear; readers do not know what you are solving |

---

## 🔴 Wording discipline

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use instead |
|---|---|
| *The deficiency of Wang's approach is…* | *However, the mechanism has not been fully understood.* |
| *The problem of these papers…* | *None of the other phases have been examined in detail.* |
| *for the first time* | State the concrete difference from prior work |
| "first proposed / first discovered / internationally advanced" | State only the objective increment |

> Every paper with a novelty can call itself "the first". Reserve "first" for **genuinely important advances**.

**Other points**
- **Never manufacture suspense** hoping for a climax at the end — readers will not stay with you
- ✅ Move forward logically: foreground the most important finding so readers advance step by step
- ⚠️ **Citing related work thoroughly does not reduce originality.** The usual effect is the opposite. Under-citing signals insufficient reading.

## Sentence bank — signalling the gap

```
Previous research (studies, records) has (have) investigated / studied (with respect to)
  failed to consider / ignored / misinterpreted / neglected to
  overestimated, underestimated / misleading
thus, these previous results are inconclusive, misleading, unsatisfactory, questionable, controversial
Uncertainties (discrepancies) still exist ...
However, little information / little attention / little work / little data / little research
  or few studies / few investigations / few researchers / few attempts / none of these studies
has (have) been less focused on / attempted to / conducted
```

## Five ways to foreground your own contribution

| # | Angle | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Time** | Establish the importance of the older, better-studied period, then note the newer timescale is under-studied |
| 2 | **Method / material property** | Present the currently popular methods and properties, then note your direction is rarely studied |
| 3 | **Region** | Summarise adjacent or other regions first, then note this region is under-studied |
| 4 | **Uncertainty** | Prior work is extensive but holds two or more competing views worth clarifying |
| 5 | **Hypothesis testing** | When no comparable prior work exists, state the hypothesis confidently: *We aim to test the feasibility (reliability) of the…* |
