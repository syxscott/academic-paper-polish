## Review article structure

A review is not a longer research paper. It has its own skeleton:

```
Title → Author → Abstract → Introduction
  → Summary of major issues in the study field
  → Model and discussion
  → Perspective on future study
→ Acknowledgement → References → Figure and table caption
```

### Three genres compared

| Dimension | **Review paper** | **Research paper** | **Perspective** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content | Evaluates previously published literature | Discusses the author's own current findings | Offers an outlook or viewpoint |
| Data | Existing research data | New experimental findings | — |
| Length | Longer than a research article | — | Journal-limited |

### Three-part introduction (review version)

| # | Element | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Subject background** | The general topic or area of concern, providing context |
| 2 | **"Problem"** | Trends, new perspectives, gaps, conflicts, or a single problem |
| 3 | **Motivation / justification** | Your reason for reviewing, plus the approach and organisation of the text |

**Specification**: present tense (past tense for your own methods and results) · dense citation · **10–20% of the core text** (introduction + body + conclusions)

## Six ways to organise subheadings

- Methodological approaches
- Models or theories
- Extent of support for a given thesis
- Studies that agree versus studies that disagree
- Chronological order
- Geographical location

**Heading depth**: subdivide as finely as possible, **at least three levels**; fourth and fifth levels are acceptable.

**Use a question as the subheading** — far more informative than "Current status of X":
- *What's the principles and mechanisms controlling the yields of ·O₂⁻, H₂O₂ and ·OH from O₂ reduction by different types of reduced species?*
- *How abiotic and biotic mechanisms co-contribute to the production of different ROS?*

## 🔴 The 18-step review workflow

**The order is counter-intuitive: body (11) → conclusions (12) → introduction (13) → abstract (14).** The introduction and abstract can only be written once the structure is settled.

| Stage | # | Step |
|---|---|---|
| prepare | 1 | Narrow the topic, define a few research questions or hypotheses |
| | 2 | Search literature sources, refining topic and questions during the search |
| | 3 | Read, evaluate, classify, and make notes |
| | 4 | Redefine the focus and research questions; define the **take-home message** |
| develop structure | 5 | Compose a preliminary title |
| | 6 | Find a structuring principle (chronological / subject matter / procedure) |
| | 7 | Prepare an outline; find headings for the text body |
| write draft | 8 | Plan the content of each paragraph |
| | 9 | Prepare tables, concept maps, figures |
| | 10 | Draft the methods section (if needed) |
| | 11 | **Draft the body sections** |
| | 12 | Draft the conclusions |
| | 13 | **Draft the introduction** |
| revise | 14 | Draft the abstract |
| | 15 | Revise sections, abstract & title, tables, figures & legends |
| | 16 | Revise citations and references |
| | 17 | Correct grammar, spelling, punctuation |
| | 18 | Adjust the layout |

## 🔴 Reference requirements

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Count** | Around **100 references** is the quality benchmark for a review |
| **Recency** | Last 5 years, ideally 1–3 years |
| **Source** | Prefer core journals, primary literature, and authoritative scholars |
| **Prohibited** | Unpublished material, textbooks, monographs as content sources |

**Five citation mistakes (check every one)**

| # | Mistake | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Citing without citing** | Borrowing a colleague's similar work but not citing it, to look "novel" |
| 2 | **Quoting out of context** | Deliberately using the part the author tried to refute, to prop up your own view |
| 3 | **Inaccurate citation** | Not reading the original; wrong or missing citation details |
| 4 | **Unreliable source** | Non-scholarly sources used without attribution; common with statistics |
| 5 | **Blind self-citation** | Citing yourself purely to raise your citation count, not to relate prior work |

## 🔴 *Earth-Science Reviews* scope note

*ESR* publishes **reviews only**. It will **not** consider bibliometric studies, meta-analyses without a significant review component, or regular research articles outside occasional special issues. Abstracts are capped at **250 words** and must be a **single paragraph**.
