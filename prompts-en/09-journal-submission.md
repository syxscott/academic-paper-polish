# Module 9 · Journal Matching & Submission

> 🔴 **Pick the journal before you write.** This determines every structural decision downstream. It is not a procedural formality.

> **How to use**: copy the code block below, replace the placeholder, send to any LLM. Self-contained.

```text
I am preparing to submit an academic paper. First help me identify the target journal, then
check my manuscript structure against it.

**Step 1 — Journal matching**
1. Based on my research content, recommend 3–5 candidate journals, ranked by fit.
2. For each candidate, state: scope boundary (does it accept my kind of study?),
   length limits, structural and formatting requirements, turnaround (desk review /
   external review / acceptance), and impact factor.
3. Identify which journals are likely to reject my work, and explain why.

**Step 2 — Structural adaptation**
According to the chosen journal's guide for authors, check my manuscript structure:
- Are Results and Discussion separated or merged?
- Is there a separate Conclusion section?
- Abstract word limit? Are Highlights required? Keyword count limit?
- Figure format, resolution, size, and width limits?
- Is there a pre-proof / article-in-press process?

**Step 3 — Submission materials checklist**
List everything the journal requires (cover letter, highlights, graphical abstract, data
availability, author contributions, ethics statement, suggested reviewers), and mark which
are mandatory.

My research: [DESCRIBE YOUR QUESTION, METHOD, AND MAIN FINDING]
My target journal (if any): [JOURNAL NAME]
```

## 🔴 Journal structural constraints

| Journal | Results/Discussion | Conclusion section | Envelope |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***GCA*** | 🔴 **Must be separated**, independently numbered | Allowed | Rejects pure methods/technical/modelling papers (needs broader geochemical significance); rejects regional case studies; cover letter **must** name 3 Associate Editors |
| ***ES&T*** | 🔴 **Merged** into Results and Discussion | ❌ *"A separate conclusion is not to be used"* | Research Article **7,000 words**; article-category structure |
| ***Paleobiology*** | — | Allowed | Review figures **<40 megapixels**; final figures **line 1200 / halftone 600 / combination 800 dpi**; single column 9 cm / double 18 cm / max height 24 cm; 🔴 **Non-technical Summary + Abstract, each ≤300 words** |
| ***Acta Palaeontologica Polonica*** | — | — | Figure parameters most precise: single column **89 mm** / double **183 mm** / max length **237 mm**; Calibri 8 pt; line 0.2 mm; scale bar 0.7 mm; photos **400 dpi**; 🔴 **ZooBank registration mandatory** |
| ***Palaeontologia Electronica*** | — | — | 🔴 **No page limit, no page charges**; figures 175 mm / 84 mm at **300 ppi**; **RGB not CMYK**; file names **ISO 9660** (≤8 chars, lowercase/digits/`_`/`.` only) |
| ***Palaeoworld*** | — | — | **≤30 printed pages**; **no page charges**; full Systematic palaeontology format |
| ***Nature*** | — | — | 6–8 printed pages; **title ≤75 characters**; main-text references **≤50**; Methods **≤3,000 words and no figures or tables** |
| ***PNAS*** | — | — | Research Report 6 pages (max 12); Brief Report 3 pages / 1,600 words; 🔴 **Significance Statement ≤120 words** |
| ***Earth-Science Reviews*** | — | — | **Reviews only**; rejects bibliometric studies; abstract ≤250 words, single paragraph |

> 🔴 **Format failure = desk rejection.** Format is the first impression, the baseline of academic literacy, and reviewers are hunting for errors. Format comments usually outnumber content comments.

## Submission review pipeline

| Work type | Pipeline |
|---|---|
| **SCI paper** | Technical check (format) → **Editorial screening (journal fit)** → Peer review |
| **Thesis** | Supervisor → **External examination** → Internal examination |

**Why format matters**: ① first impression ② baseline academic literacy ③ reviewing is error-hunting ④ **format comments usually outnumber content comments**

## Front-matter conventions

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Title | Accurate, informative, **avoid abbreviations**; core keywords first |
| Article header | **Sentence case** (first word capitalised), not Title Case For Every Word |
| Abstract | ~300 words; no abbreviations or references |
| Keywords | **<6**, avoid repeating words from the title |
| Highlights | **<5 bullets, ≤85 characters each** |
| Heading levels | `1` → `1.1` → `1.1.1`; **avoid excessive nesting** |
| Line numbers | 🔴 **Continuous line numbers** (submission check item) |
| Layout | **12 pt, double-spaced, continuous line numbers, page numbers** |

## Cover letter

**Core question: why is this manuscript suitable for your journal?**

***GCA* hard requirements**
1. A cover letter is **mandatory** for every submission
2. Explain fit with the journal's aim: *to present studies of fundamental significance and broad relevance for understanding geochemical systems, mechanisms, and processes…*
3. Disclose any **potential conflicts of interest** the editor should know about
4. You may request that specific reviewers or editors be excluded, but **in GCA this is purely advisory and non-binding**
5. 🔴 **You must supply the names of 3 Associate Editors**

***Acta Palaeontologica Polonica***: no longer than one side of A4 (**≤300 words**); the first paragraph explains why the research was needed and states the question clearly.

## Post-submission states

| State | Meaning and typical duration |
|---|---|
| Submitted to Journal | Awaiting initial check and reviewer assignment; usually fast |
| With editor | Editor decides whether to send for external review; **days to weeks** |
| Under Review / Peer Review | Reviewer assessment; **weeks to months — the longest phase** |
| Decision in Process | Editor decides; corresponding author receives the Decision letter |
| Revise | Revision and re-submission. Major or Minor |
| Accepted / Rejected | Accepted → production; rejected |

---

## Outcome probabilities (know the expectations in advance)

| Outcome | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Rejected** | In many journals **one** rejecting reviewer is enough for the editor to reject |
| **Major revision** | Substantial work needed; if done well, usually accepted |
| **Minor revision** | Small fixes; **effectively accept** |
| **Accepted** | Outright acceptance is rare; there are usually some revisions |

**Formatted desk-rejection phrasing** (recognise it; no reply needed):
1. *"We regret to say that…"*
2. *"We suggest that you might submit it to another journal"*
3. *"An increasing volume of submissions prevents us from publishing all worthwhile manuscripts we receive…"*
